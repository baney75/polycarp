Reading that fits the way you think

Polycarp sets any page, PDF or ebook in the type that is easiest for you, reads it aloud and highlights each word as it goes, and helps you keep what you read.

1Pick how you read

A starting point. Change anything later in Settings and Polycarp keeps your mix.

2Three things to try

  1. Any web page Polycarp is already on. Press Alt Shift P to turn it off or on for a site, or click the Polycarp icon for quick changes.
  2. A PDF, ebook or Word file Open it in the Reader for clean pages, contents, printing and recall breaks. On a PDF tab, press Alt Shift R.
  3. Listen and remember In the Reader, press L to hear it read aloud with each word highlighted. Every few pages a short recall break plants the main ideas, and Review brings them back as they start to fade.

Why it works

Polycarp is built on reading and memory research. Here is what each part rests on and how strong the evidence is. Where it is weak, we say so.

    What Polycarp does not promise

    Photographic memory. Careful study has not found it in adults (Haber 1979). What does work is recalling what you read, spaced over days. Polycarp builds that into every document, so more of what you read stays with you.

    Speed reading. Reading much faster than normal costs understanding (Rayner et al. 2016). Polycarp aims for reading that is easier, steadier and better remembered.

    What is new in

    With the Polycarp app installed, this extension updates with it. When something changes, it is listed here.

    • One Polycarp in your browser. The reading extension and Polycarp's new tab are now one extension, in Polycarp's own colors and type. Restore a backup from the earlier reading extension in Settings and it carries over.
    • Polycarp Search. One box for your library, YouTube, Wikipedia and free book catalogs. Paste any link to make that site, feed or catalog searchable too, and make your Audible or Kindle library searchable from its own page.
    • Your own NAS. Browse it, search its files, save downloads to it and keep a daily backup there.
    • Find anything on a page. Press Alt Shift S for headings, links, images and video, a YouTube transcript, or the X posts you have scrolled past.
    • Take a break. Pause Polycarp for an hour or until tomorrow from the toolbar popup, or switch off any site helper in Settings.
    • A library that links itself. Save pages, PDFs, ebooks, audiobooks and notes. Polycarp connects them by shared ideas, people and sites, and by the [[links]] in your notes. Send it all to Obsidian or Notion.
    • A Reader for many formats. PDF, EPUB, DOCX, HTML, Markdown, FB2 and plain text, with contents, a library and your place saved.
    • Listen with word highlighting. Natural Kokoro voices that run on this computer, with a second voice for dialogue. They download once, the first time you listen.
    • Recall breaks and a memory garden. Short questions while you read, and spaced review that brings each idea back as it starts to fade.
    • Print it your way. Your font, spacing and anchors on paper, with an optional recall sheet at the end.
    • Make every site readable. A minimum text size, left-aligned text, no italics, and copy turned back on where sites block it.

    Private by design

    Polycarp has no account, no tracking and no servers. Your settings, library, notes and memory garden stay on this computer. Polycarp goes online only when you ask: to open a page or file, to search (only your search words go to the sources you switch on), to download the reading voice the first time you listen, or to reach a NAS you connect. If you connect Notion, your library goes to Notion when you press Sync, and not before.
