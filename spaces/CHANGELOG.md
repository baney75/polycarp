## Unreleased

- **A closed window's space fills at once.** A window closed with its button or ⌘W, minimized, moved to another workspace, or whose app quit stayed in its tile until a scan saw the window server drop it, and when the window server kept it a moment longer nothing looked again, so the others never grew to fill the space. Now the window leaves its workspace the moment its app says so, and tiling looks at once; a closed tab still hands its tile to the tab behind it. spec/tiling-speed.md R-TS8. Pinned by `LeaveNowWiringTests` and `CloseFillMovesTests`. Untested live. Linux and Windows: unchanged.
- **The bar's battery looks like macOS's own.** A 23 by 11 pt body with a hairline outline and round corners, a small nub, and a fill inset inside it in the bar's text color. Charging never tints it, Low Power Mode included: the theme's `working` color shows only in Low Power Mode when not charging, and `urgent` only at 10% or below on battery. The bolt and the plug are cut cleanly out of the fill, the glyph sits on whole pixels so its outline stays one pixel at 1x, and it keeps the room either side that the symbols beside it have, so the status icons are evenly spaced. The outline is as light as macOS's, or as much darker as reaching 3:1 needs in your theme. `bar.batteryPercent` (off by default) shows the charge in digits beside it. The tray's chevron is a step smaller and no heavier than the icons. spec/bar.md R-BB1 to R-BB5. Pinned by `BatteryGlyphTests`; `Polycarp --render` draws battery-1x.png and battery-2x.png. Untested live. Windows: no battery yet.
- **Tiling times each close and move.** A tiled window closed, minimized, quit or sent to another workspace, and a tiling move key or drop, writes a `close` or `move` line to timing.log: how long until the last frame for the other windows came back (`fill=`), and how many frames each window got. `scripts/speed-report.sh` holds them to the new `close-fill` and `move-fill` budgets. spec/tiling-speed.md R-TS10, R-TS11. Pinned by `TileActionsTests`, `WindowLeftWiringTests` and `SpeedBudgetTests`. Untested live. Linux and Windows: unchanged.
- **`polycarp browser install` and `update` show how far they are.** In a terminal one line redraws: a bar, the percent, bytes over the total and the speed while Helium downloads, then each step by name (Download, Verify, Build, Sign, Check, Swap in, step N of 6) with a spinner and a clock, and a finished step leaves a line with the time it took. Piped or in a log, each step is a plain line with no escape codes. Pinned by the cli's `progress` tests and `every_step_is_shown_in_order_and_the_last_line_names_the_version`. Linux and Windows: unchanged.
- **Tiling hears a window open or close from its app.** Polycarp now listens for each app's own notes that a window was created, closed, minimized or brought back, and scans that app at once; before, a new or closed window reached tiling only when something else (the focus moving, an app switching) started a scan. The `tile=new` and `tile=close` lines in timing.log time from those notes. spec/tiling-speed.md R-TS6. Pinned by `WindowNotesWiringTests`. Linux and Windows: unchanged.
- **Arrange Bar Items, for moving modules without ⌘.** Right-click the top bar's empty space or the mark and choose Arrange Bar Items…: every module is outlined, the island splits show as grips that move too, a plain drag moves any of them and clicks open nothing. Windows: not yet.

Importing saves a look without putting it on, and one of yours is replaced only after you say so.
- **Delete a saved look.** `polycarp look delete <name>` removes it; the look in force needs `--force`, and how things look doesn't change.
- **Undo a look.** Each `polycarp look apply` keeps how everything looked before, the newest ten; `polycarp look undo` puts it back byte for byte, and undo again comes forward. A theme the look brought is taken away again unless you edited it.
- **Put a whole look on at once.** `polycarp look apply <name|file>` changes the theme, `style.css`, the surfaces' looks and the paper together: everything is checked first, and if a step fails what came before it is put back and the step is named. `apply polycarp` is everything as Polycarp comes. Your own modules, search engine and theme files stay, and a sheet you wrote by hand goes to a backup. `--dry-run` only says what would change.
- **Keep how everything looks under a name.** `polycarp look save <name>` keeps the theme, mode, accents, `style.css`, the surfaces' looks and the paper as one look that needs no other file. Your `custom:` modules stay out of it, and saving over a look needs `--force`.
- **A look says what it would change.** `polycarp look show` lists in plain words what differs from how everything looks now ("bar.height: 32 (now its default)"), and `list` marks the look in force.
- **Polycarp's own checks run before a look is kept.** `polycarp look import` has `polycarp-spaces style check` and `surfaces validate` read the look's sheet and settings, and refuses what they refuse, including a setting this build doesn't know. Without Polycarp Spaces it says those parts were not checked.
- **A look carries its paper.** Each place's paper is a color, a scene or a picture inside the look file. A picture is checked by its own bytes, type, size and sha256, and one no paper uses is refused.
- **A look holds the style sheet and how the surfaces are drawn.** Its settings come from one list in `cli/src/look/fields.rs`, with every field of surfaces.json in or out for a stated reason: bar height, style and modules, the desktop scene and the idle style are in; search engines, URLs, files, the new tab question and anything else that isn't appearance are dropped with a note, as are `custom:` modules.

Nothing is replaced without `--force`, or read or written through a link.
